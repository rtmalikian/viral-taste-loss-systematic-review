# Pre-submission Peer Review — R3

**Persona:** R3 — ENT / chemosensory clinician (gustation, olfaction and flavour; clinical applicability)
**Date:** 2026-11-21
**Manuscript reviewed:** `manuscript.md` v1.0, 21 November 2026 — *Loss of taste in possible viral infection: etiologies, pathogenesis, and the evidence for treatments*
**Sources checked against:** manuscript text; `analysis/synthesis/q2_pathogenesis_synthesis.md`; `analysis/synthesis/q3_treatment_synthesis.md`
**Overall recommendation:** **Major revision — do not submit in current form.** The gustation-versus-flavour distinction is correctly defined and the taste-separability rule is the review's strongest design choice, and no sentence constitutes a direct clinical or medication recommendation. Two claims about olfactory-mediated misattribution (T4) nevertheless contradict or overstate the review's own cited evidence, the practice conclusions reintroduce a longitudinal reading the Results explicitly forbid, and the special-population objective is not delivered. All are fixable without new analyses except where a subgroup/sensitivity statement is called for.

**Severity counts:** Blocker 2 · Major 6 · Minor 5 · Nit 2 · Total 15

---

## Blockers

**R3-01 — Blocker — Discussion, "Overall completeness and applicability" ("122 of the 137 frequency studies used self-report, which the T4 evidence shows overstates gustatory loss…")**
Justification: The claim of net overstatement is contradicted by the manuscript's own pools — psychophysical testing pooled higher (47.4%, 15 studies) than self-report (36.5%, 122 studies) — and the "Agreements and disagreements" paragraph itself notes this is "the opposite of what a simple misattribution story would predict," so the Discussion cannot simultaneously assert that T4 *shows* self-report overstates gustatory loss at population level.

**R3-02 — Blocker — Results, Q2 T4 paragraph; Plain language summary ("most direct testing" / "purpose-built objective-testing studies")**
Justification: C4367 is characterised in the Q2 synthesis (§3, T4) as a prospective-cohort *survey* of whether respondents could differentiate basic tastes (60% at first survey, 88.9% at follow-up), not a psychophysical objective test, and C11888's Taste Strips result rests on only 30 tested participants (Q2 synthesis §3) — a denominator the manuscript omits — so grouping all three studies as purpose-built objective testing overstates the directness and precision of the T4 base.

## Majors

**R3-03 — Major — Abstract, Main results; Results, Q2 T5 paragraph**
Justification: T5 is presented as having direct human taste evidence, but per Q2 synthesis §3/§4 its four direct-human studies were measured largely against composite or paired outcomes — R00084 against olfactory *and* gustatory dysfunction, R01294 in olfactory/taste-dysfunction subjects, and R00066 in dysosmia/dysgeusia cases — so the taste-specific directness is weaker than the manuscript's wording implies, and the manuscript itself concedes the taste-bud-turnover half of T5 is measured in no included human study.

**R3-04 — Major — Background, theory taxonomy; Abstract and Discussion references to T4 as a pathogenesis mechanism**
Justification: T4 is listed flatly among eleven pathogenesis theories without the category caveat stated in Q2 synthesis §3 — T4 is a claim about measurement and attribution ("what the symptom report means"), not a tissue mechanism, and competes with T1/T2/T5 only at that level — so a clinical reader is invited to weigh it as a biological mechanism on the same scale as direct infection or inflammation.

**R3-05 — Major — Authors' conclusions, "Implications for practice" ("most people's taste recovered completely over the following weeks to months")**
Justification: The phrasing reads as a within-patient trajectory, which the Results and Figure 3 legend explicitly forbid — the three complete-recovery windows (87.4% ≤4 weeks, 85.2% >4–12 weeks, 87.8% >12 weeks) rest on *different sets of studies* and "must not be read as a within-cohort trajectory" — and the practice paragraph reintroduces exactly that reading for the audience least able to discount it.

**R3-06 — Major — Results, Q1 recovery; Discussion, "Quality of the evidence"**
Justification: Frequency is subgrouped by ascertainment (psychophysical 47.4% vs self-report 36.5%), but the recovery pools carry no equivalent ascertainment subgroup or sensitivity statement, even though the same flavour-versus-gustation confounding that motivates the frequency subgroup applies to self-reported "complete recovery," leaving the taste-specific label on the recovery estimates less secure than on the frequency estimate.

**R3-07 — Major — Discussion, applicability; Results, Q1 (no age applicability limit)**
Justification: No statement limits the pooled frequency (37.7%) or recovery estimates by age and no paediatric-separable estimate is reported, despite paediatric studies sitting in the included set (e.g. Appendix C: R00884, paediatric SARS-CoV-2; R00648, children; R00574, children and adults) and despite age-dependent taste reporting and psychophysical test performance being a routine ENT concern in children — the pooled figures will be read as applying to children without warrant.

**R3-08 — Major — Objectives (Q3 "special-population considerations"); Results, Q3 final paragraph; Discussion (silence)**
Justification: The pre-specified special-population objective is discharged in a single Q3 sentence and never returned to in the Discussion, omitting material already in the syntheses: cerebrolysin trial eligibility at ages 10–70 with no results (Q3 synthesis §12), the R00334 survey finding that only respondents under 40 reported more than slight efficacy of steroids or smell training (Q3 synthesis §11), and any Q1/Q2 applicability statement for the pregnant-women cohort in the included set (Appendix C: R00129, long COVID in pregnant women).

## Minors

**R3-09 — Minor — Methods, Eligibility criteria (ageusia, hypogeusia, dysgeusia "including parageusia and phantogeusia")**
Justification: No operational rule is stated for separating gustatory parageusia/phantogeusia from olfactory parosmia/phantosmia, which present identically in patient language ("tastes wrong / tastes of something that isn't there"), so the taste-separability rule is under-specified for precisely the distortion phenotypes where ENT misclassification is most likely.

**R3-10 — Minor — Background, flavour definition; Results, Q2 T11 paragraph**
Justification: Flavour is correctly defined as including trigeminal sensation, yet chemesthesis is never separated in eligibility or outcomes and the one trigeminal finding in the set (C4631, prolonged masseter inhibitory reflex latencies; Q2 synthesis §3, T11) is buried in the residual T11 list as "trigeminal electrophysiology" with no clinical interpretation, leaving a component of what patients call "taste" unexamined.

**R3-11 — Minor — Results, Q3 photobiomodulation paragraph; Plain language summary**
Justification: The manuscript reports the single-session design, 36 participants, 1-month follow-up and unreported adverse events, but omits the enrolment frame given in Q3 synthesis §3 — persistent taste loss ≥4 weeks — so a reader cannot tell that the only positive randomised signal does not address acute-phase taste loss.

**R3-12 — Minor — Summary of findings, Table 2; Results, Q3 (doses as printed) — recommendation-adjacent presentation**
Justification: Studied doses are correctly reported descriptively and are *not* recommendations (prednisolone 40 mg/day for 10 days; zinc sulfate 220 mg twice daily; pregabalin 25 mg every 12 hours in the single case, R01519), but Table 2 and the Q3 text carry no adjacent "studied dose, not a recommended dose" flag, so a skim-reading clinician could lift a dose — notably the zinc dose — out of a table in a review whose practice section must be recommendation-free.

**R3-13 — Minor — Plain language summary, treatments paragraph ("looked promising")**
Justification: "A light treatment applied to the tongue (photobiomodulation) looked promising in one small trial of 36 people" is not a recommendation and is immediately qualified ("one small trial is not enough to rely on"), but "promising" is endorsement-adjacent language for a lay audience for an intervention with no adverse-event reporting in that trial (Q3 synthesis §3), and should be neutralised to match the Abstract's and practice section's non-recommending register.

## Nits

**R3-14 — Nit — Authors' conclusions, "Implications for practice" ("roughly a third to a half of patients")**
Justification: The verbal range tracks the confidence interval around 37.7% (95% CI 32.9 to 42.7) rather than the prediction interval (5.3 to 86.7%) that the same paragraph and the Discussion say governs what can be said to a patient in a particular setting; the parenthetical PI mitigates but does not remove the mismatch between the spoken range and the stated uncertainty standard.

**R3-15 — Nit — Results, Q2 T1 paragraph; Background (quality-specific loss)**
Justification: Quality-specific findings in the cited evidence — e.g. R01523's divergent thresholds (sweet/sour/bitter up, salty down; Q2 synthesis §4) and R00044's bitter-taste-dysfunction population — are never drawn out clinically, so the review's uniform ageusia/hypogeusia framing misses the ENT-relevant point that viral gustatory loss in the tested studies is not uniform across the five basic tastes the Background correctly enumerates.

## Remit-specific verdicts (for the gate record)

- **Gustation versus flavour framing:** Definition in Background is clinically correct (five basic tastes; flavour = gustation + retronasal smell + texture, temperature and trigeminal sensation) and is the manuscript's framing strength; execution slips are R3-01, R3-09, R3-10 and R3-15.
- **Taste-separability eligibility rule:** Sound in design (separate/extractable taste required; taste-ambiguous flag resolved at full text; composite smell-or-taste kept in a labelled separate stratum, pooled at 34.2% in 27 studies and never merged) and honestly stress-tested in the Methods (discarded Screener B implementations at kappa ~0.07 disclosed); residual gaps are the recovery-pool ascertainment gap (R3-06) and the distortion-phenotype gap (R3-09). The handling of composite-only treatment outcomes is correct: R01955 (zinc) is rightly recorded as having *no* taste-specific estimate, with its 7- vs 18-day durations identified as smell recovery only.
- **T4 claims versus cited studies:** Counts and directions for R02414 (Taste Strips normal in all tested complainers), C11888 (normal in 86.7–96.7% per taste quality) and the R00232 counterweight (23.5% objectively hypogeusic) match Q2 synthesis §3; the failures are evidence-type inflation for C4367 and the omitted C11888 denominator (R3-02) and the net-overstatement inference (R3-01). The manuscript is also correct, and should keep, its caution that T4 studies retain a minority with genuine gustatory deficits — T4 must not be rewritten as "all reported taste loss is smell loss."
- **Clinical / medication recommendations — explicit check:** **None found.** No sentence recommends starting, stopping, choosing or dosing any intervention for a patient. The explicit non-recommendations are present and correctly placed (Abstract Authors' conclusions; Plain language summary final paragraph; Discussion; "Implications for practice" opening). The caution "Clinicians should not carry the COVID-19 frequencies across to influenza" is an applicability caution, not a treatment recommendation, and should be retained. Only presentation risks remain: R3-12 and R3-13.
- **Clinical applicability statements:** The SARS-CoV-2-only limit, the ban on borrowing figures for influenza, the descriptive (non-claimed) hospitalised (26.8%) versus community (39.6%) subgroup presentation with no interaction test, and the harms-reporting caveat ("a zero event count… is evidence about reporting, not about safety") are all clinically sound; the unsound or incomplete applicability items are R3-01, R3-05, R3-07, R3-08 and R3-11. One scope note, not scored as an issue: the practice section offers no triage statement (e.g. persistent, unilateral or neurologically accompanied taste loss prompting non-viral workup); if the author regards triage as out of scope for an etiology/treatment review, a single scoping sentence saying so would close the gap for ENT readers.
- **Special populations:** Children — inadequate (R3-07, R3-08): treatment coverage is correctly stated as one adolescent series with no taste-specific outcome (R00782, ages 12–17 per Q3 synthesis §12), but frequency/recovery applicability to children is unaddressed. Pregnancy — partially adequate: exclusion in the prednisolone (C9634) and photobiomodulation (R00627) trials and absence of any dose adjustment are correctly reported, but no applicability statement covers pregnancy for Q1/Q2 (R3-08). Older adults — inadequate: the only older-adult treatment datum is the n=1 pregabalin case at age 87 (R01519), which the manuscript reports, but age-stratified recovery, polypharmacy/dentition confounding, and the R00334 age-efficacy gradient are all absent from the applicability discussion (R3-08).
