## The point verified

**Loop 1 position.** The PLS pathogenesis paragraph listed only three explanations as those with the most direct testing behind them — the smell (olfactory-misattribution) explanation, inflammation, and direct infection of taste tissue — with no mention of central gustatory pathway involvement (T9). That put the PLS out of step with the four-theory headline in the Abstract ("systemic or local inflammation, olfactory-mediated misattribution, direct infection of taste tissue, and central gustatory pathway involvement") and in the Discussion Summary ("the coherence-adjusted best support in the inflammation, misattribution, direct-infection and central-pathway theories"). The Loop 1 recommended fix was a one-line addition of the central/brain-pathway explanation, qualified as resting on a single brain-imaging study.

**Loop 2 text (PLS, pathogenesis paragraph, `manuscript/manuscript.md`).** After the sentence listing the three explanations and its evidence clause ("…in several studies, people who said they had lost taste scored normally on taste tests, and a few studies measured inflammation markers or examined tissue"), the paragraph now adds:

> "One brain-imaging study also directly tested the idea that the brain's own taste pathways are involved."

**Judgement — in step with the headline: yes.** Read as a whole, the PLS paragraph now covers all four headline theories: smell/misattribution, inflammation, direct infection of taste tissue, and the brain's own (central) taste pathways. A plain-language reader is no longer given a three-theory account that contradicts the four-theory ranking stated in the Abstract and Discussion. The placement as a separate "also" sentence, rather than insertion into the "most direct testing" list itself, does not leave a residual discrepancy: the sentence states that the central idea was directly tested, and the "also" plus the singular study count appropriately signal that its direct basis is thinner than that of the other three — which is the accurate position, since T9's place in the headline is a coherence judgement, not raw-count parity.

**Judgement — T9's support not overstated: checked against Table 3 and Results–Q2.**

- Table 3, T9 row: 28 studies tagged, 2 direct-human, 1 indirect-human, 22 speculative, 3 mixed as stated.
- Results–Q2 accounts for the two direct-human tags distinctly: one genuinely direct imaging study (diffusion MRI of taste-related brain regions over four years, R00185) and a second direct-human study of decreased vividness of taste mental imagery (R01073, patient-reported).
- The PLS sentence claims exactly **one** brain-imaging study — singular "One … study" — which is R00185 and nothing else. It does not claim two imaging studies, does not convert the patient-reported imagery study (R01073) into imaging evidence, and does not inflate the 2 direct-human tags into 2 genuinely direct tests.
- The wording is calibrated in a second respect: the study "directly tested the idea that the brain's own taste pathways are involved" — a claim of a direct test of involvement, not of proof, causation, or established mechanism. That matches the Abstract's own qualifier that "the central theory rests on one genuinely direct imaging study."

The added sentence therefore does precisely what Loop 1 asked for: it brings the PLS into step with the four-theory headline, with the single-imaging-study qualification intact and no overstatement of T9's support.
