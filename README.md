# Loss of taste in possible viral infection: etiologies, pathogenesis, and the evidence for treatments

## A Cochrane-level systematic review and meta-analysis where poolable

**Raphael Malikian** (rtmalikian@gmail.com), Palmdale, CA, USA. ORCID [0009-0008-4326-4205](https://orcid.org/0009-0008-4326-4205).
Independent researcher. This review is written from a family medicine perspective.

## Headline findings

- 5,988 records retrieved; 3,501 screened; **363 studies included** (362 unique publications).
- Acute taste dysfunction in SARS-CoV-2 pooled at **37.7%** (95% CI 32.9 to 42.7; 137 studies, 155,010 participants), with extreme heterogeneity (I² 99.5%; 95% prediction interval 5.3 to 86.7%). Every pooled study concerns SARS-CoV-2 — no study of influenza or any other named virus mapped to any pool.
- Complete taste recovery pooled at **87.4%** within 4 weeks, **85.2%** at more than 4 to 12 weeks, and **87.8%** beyond 12 weeks, each from a different study set. Certainty (GRADE) is very low for most outcomes.
- Pathogenesis: 214 studies carried a mechanism code; 73.4% were speculative (proposed, not measured) and only 15 carried direct human evidence.
- Treatments: only **23 studies tested any intervention** and only 2 randomised trials reported a taste-outcome estimate. Prednisolone showed little to no difference in the one low-risk-of-bias trial; photobiomodulation rests on a single 36-participant trial; zinc has no taste-specific effect estimate anywhere. **This review makes no recommendation for or against any treatment.**
