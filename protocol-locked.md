## Review questions
Q1 Etiology/frequency: which confirmed or possible viral infections are associated with new-onset taste dysfunction, with what reported frequency, timing, duration and recovery?
Q2 Pathogenesis: what mechanisms are proposed and what is the directness/quality of evidence for each (pre-specified theory list below)?
Q3 Treatments: for every intervention studied for viral-associated taste dysfunction, effects on recovery, studied dose/course/duration, harms, and special-population considerations.

## Eligibility (locked)
Population: Humans, any age, any setting, with new-onset ageusia, hypogeusia or dysgeusia (including parageusia/phantogeusia when reported as taste dysfunction) attributed to or temporally following a confirmed, probable or possible viral infection (laboratory-confirmed, clinically diagnosed, or self-reported viral illness / upper-respiratory infection where no non-viral cause is established). Taste must be reported separately OR extractably distinct from smell/flavour; records where 'taste' is used but only olfaction was measured are INCLUDED at title/abstract (flagged TASTE-AMBIGUOUS) and resolved at full text — they are not excluded at screening.
Interventions/Exposures: Q1/Q2 any viral etiology or mechanistic investigation (including human tissue, and — for Q2 only, in a separate stratum — animal/in-vitro work explicitly about viral taste pathogenesis). Q3 any intervention (drug, supplement, training/rehabilitation, device/neuromodulation, procedure, or no treatment/placebo/usual care comparator) started for the taste dysfunction.
Comparators: Q3 placebo, no treatment, usual care, another intervention, or pre-post (uncontrolled designs included but analysed separately and never pooled with RCTs).
Outcomes — Critical: (1) proportion with complete taste recovery at a stated time point; (2) proportion with any (complete or partial) recovery; (3) time to recovery; (4) any adverse event, serious adverse event, and discontinuation due to adverse events (Q3).
Outcomes — Important: change in validated gustatory test score (e.g. taste strips, whole-mouth suprathreshold, electrogustometry); validated patient-reported taste score; quality of life; proportion with persistent dysfunction at longest follow-up (≥12 weeks stratum); smell–taste discordance.
Study designs: RCTs and quasi-RCTs (Q3 primary); prospective/retrospective cohort, case-control, cross-sectional with denominator (Q1/Q3 secondary); case series ≥10 participants (included, descriptive only); mechanistic human studies (Q2). Case reports <10, narrative reviews, editorials, letters without original data: EXCLUDED from synthesis but reviews are retained for citation chasing. Conference abstracts with extractable data: included, flagged.
Exclusions: taste dysfunction wholly explained by chemotherapy, radiotherapy, head/neck surgery or trauma, neurodegenerative disease, or a named non-viral drug cause, with no separable viral subgroup; congenital taste disorders; animal/in-vitro studies not about viral pathogenesis (for Q2 stratum); duplicates; records with neither title nor abstract information on taste or virus (screening stage).
Language/date: No date limit. English-language full texts in the primary synthesis; non-English records are logged as 'awaiting classification' at screening and never silently dropped.

## Pre-specified pathogenesis theory taxonomy (Q2)
T1 Direct infection/injury of taste bud / taste receptor cells ( incl. ACE2/TMPRSS2 or other viral-entry expression in taste epithelium); T2 Sustentacular/supporting-cell or lingual epithelium injury without direct receptor-cell infection; T3 Cranial nerve involvement (chorda tympani VII, IX, X — neuropathy/inflammation); T4 Olfactory-mediated misattribution (retronasal flavour loss reported as 'taste'); T5 Systemic/local inflammation and cytokine effects on taste bud turnover; T6 Salivary gland infection/dysfunction altering tastant delivery; T7 Micronutrient/zinc pathway disruption; T8 Vascular/microthrombotic or hypoxic injury; T9 Central gustatory pathway involvement; T10 Autoimmune/post-viral immune-mediated injury; T11 Other (specify). Each study may map to multiple theories; evidence graded direct-human / indirect-human / animal / in-vitro / speculative.

## Subgroups (pre-specified, interaction tests required for any subgroup claim)
Virus: SARS-CoV-2 vs influenza vs other named virus vs unspecified/possible viral. Confirmation: laboratory-confirmed vs clinical/self-report. Duration at enrolment: acute <4 wk vs persistent 4–12 wk vs >12 wk. Age: children (<18) vs adults vs older adults (≥65, where reported). Treatment class (Q3). Pregnancy, immunocompromise, diabetes: descriptive only unless ≥2 studies per stratum.
Sensitivity analyses: excluding high overall RoB; excluding preprints; excluding TASTE-AMBIGUOUS records reclassified at full text as olfaction-only; excluding case series/uncontrolled designs from any pooled estimate.

## Statistics (locked)
Random-effects meta-analysis (restricted maximum likelihood tau², Hartung–Knapp adjustment) for proportions (logit transformation) and for comparative effects (risk ratio for recovery; mean difference / standardised mean difference for scores) where ≥2 studies map to the fixed outcome definitions and time windows (≤4 wk, >4–12 wk, >12 wk). Report tau², I² and 95% prediction intervals. Studies that do not map are narrative, never forced. One pool hand-checked in full by the statistician; crude totals of every pool checked against the extraction sheet. Analysis code and datasets published with the manuscript.

## Risk of bias & GRADE
RoB 2 (RCTs), ROBINS-I (non-randomised treatment/cohort), JBI checklist for case series/cross-sectional where neither fits (named as the tool used, not adapted silently). All in duplicate, disagreements adjudicated by reading. GRADE per critical outcome; Summary of Findings table placed before Background in the manuscript.
Expected GRADE pressure points (named in advance): dominance of self-reported taste outcomes (indirectness/measurement bias); confounding by spontaneous recovery in uncontrolled designs; imprecision for non-COVID viruses and for most treatments; publication bias where trial registration is absent.

## Special populations (extraction fields, locked)
Pregnancy/lactation; children/adolescents; older adults/frailty; immunocompromised; diabetes; chronic kidney/liver disease; polypharmacy/interaction-relevant comorbidity; pre-existing chemosensory disorders. For each treatment: any dose adjustment, contraindication, or adverse-event difference reported in the source papers — reported as evidence, never converted into a recommendation.

## Background citation standard — BOTH papers (added at lock, Raphael 2026-11-20)
- Backgrounds are built primarily on independent external evidence, in this order: existing systematic reviews/meta-analyses and clinical guidelines on viral/post-viral taste dysfunction first, then large RCTs / large cohorts, then primary mechanistic studies for pathogenesis claims.
- Evidence tiers (author's 2026-10-23 standard): Tier 1 = SR/MA/guideline; Tier 2 = RCT/large cohort; Tier 3 = small observational/pilot; Tier 4 = never load-bearing and labelled as such where cited.
- Every Background claim traces to a verified citation actually retrieved and read in full — no invented citations or DOIs.
- The Project 1 self-citation in Project 2 is one citation among that external base, never the sole support for any claim.
- All competing reviews found in this search are cited and differentiated in both Backgrounds. Capture for this: screening/competing_reviews_and_guidelines.csv — 179 records whose titles identify them as systematic/scoping/narrative reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines or consensus documents, extracted from the 3501 deduplicated records on 2026-11-20; at the Background-writing stage each is read and either cited/differentiated or logged as not relevant with a reason (title match alone does not guarantee relevance — e.g. reviews of adjacent conditions are in the file).
