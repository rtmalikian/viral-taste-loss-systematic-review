# Q3 Treatment Synthesis — Loss of taste in viral infection (systematic review, Raphael Malikian)

**Source rule:** Every statement below traces to a row of `q3_treatment_table.csv`, which was compiled solely from `extraction/extraction_settled_full.csv` (the only extraction source), with adjudicated risk of bias from `analysis/rob/rob_adjudicated_full.csv` and effect numbers checked against `fulltexts_text/<record_id>.txt` where the settled effect field was ambiguous (R00334, R00782, R01311, R01955, R02506, R01391 — see the table's `fulltext_verification_note` column). No number has been converted, pooled, or invented. No treatment recommendation is made anywhere in this synthesis.

**Count note (verified by code):** The settled file contains **23 studies with a non-blank intervention field, and R00334 is one of those 23** — it is not a 24th distinct study. The table therefore has **23 rows (one per distinct study)**, with R00334 carried as a separately flagged row category ("treatment-use survey, no effect estimate"). A 24th distinct treatment study does not exist in the settled set; C8424 is excluded (FLAG-AUTHOR) and has a blank intervention field in the settled file, and it is absent from the table. Table IDs match the settled intervention set exactly (code-verified).

**Population frame:** All 23 studies concern SARS-CoV-2. No treatment study of a non-COVID virus is in the settled intervention set. Timing splits into acute-phase treatment (R00323 [acute-phase drugs, outcomes in a ≥30-day cohort], R01388, R01391, R01955, R02506, R02693, and subject 2 of R01378) and treatment of persistent dysfunction (the remaining studies; enrolment from >4 weeks to a mean of 29.6 months after onset).

## 1. Corticosteroids (alone, combined, or within bundles)

- **Randomised evidence — 1 trial, with results:** C9634 (COCOS, double-blind placebo-controlled RCT; RoB 2 overall **Low**). Oral prednisolone 40 mg once daily for 10 days plus olfactory training vs matching placebo plus olfactory training (58 vs 57 randomised). At 12 weeks there was **no effect on gustatory function**: median Taste Strip Test total 11 (IQR 9–13) vs 11 (IQR 9.3–13), difference 0.00 (95% CI −1.00 to 1.00), p=0.50; taste VAS difference 0.1 (95% CI −1.00 to 1.3), p=0.80. The cohort was sampled on persistent olfactory disorders; gustatory function was a secondary outcome. Adverse events are fully reported for this trial (see §10).
- **Uncontrolled pre-post only:** R00232 (oral prednisolone 10 mg twice daily + zinc gluconate 10 mg three times daily, 1 month then a further 1–3 months; ROBINS-I **Serious**). In the smell-and-taste group (n=34), phone follow-up at mean 10.35 ± 5.50 months reported complete gustatory restoration in 18 (52.9%), partial in 12, none in 4 (improvement rate 88.2%); the retested subgroup (n=18) WETT rose 26.28 ± 7.13 to 28.61 ± 8.38 (p=0.019), while the hypogeusic-at-retest subgroup (n=3) showed 0/3 improvement. Direction (improvement over time) cannot be separated from spontaneous recovery in this design.
- **Non-randomised controlled (bundle, steroids are one component among several):** R01388 (ROBINS-I **Serious**) — smell/taste training plus intranasal corticosteroid sprays, oral vitamin B complex with omega-3, oral vitamin A and intranasal sodium citrate (systemic corticosteroids were not prescribed) vs conventional COVID-19 management only: recovery to normal taste 88.6% (study, n=35 with taste dysfunction) vs 63.9% (control, n=36), mean duration of reduced taste ~6 vs ~8 weeks. The steroid component cannot be isolated from the bundle.
- **Uncontrolled combination protocol:** R00356 (ROBINS-I **Serious**) — dexamethasone then prednisone within a multi-drug protocol (mesoglycan, acetazolamide, vitamin complex, omeprazole), n=87, single arm: mean taste VAS 3.2 at treatment start to 7.1 at 12 months (p<0.001 between time points); no complete-recovery proportion printed.
- **Observational exposure only (no assigned steroid treatment):** R00323 (systemic corticosteroids among self-chosen acute-phase drugs, n=146 exposed; see §8), R01391 (intranasal steroids n=312; systemic steroids n=62 in routine care; see §8), R00574 (local and systemic steroids among treatments received; paper states no modality was effective, no per-treatment rates), R00782 (nasal corticosteroids given to 13/18 adolescents as clinical care; no taste-specific outcome), R02693 (prednisone and triamcinolone appear only in phase 2 of the TNT bundle; see §7), R00334 (nasal/oral steroids in the flagged survey; see §11).

**Consistency of direction:** The only randomised, low-RoB estimate (C9634) shows no gustatory effect of oral prednisolone; all positive steroid-associated findings come from uncontrolled pre-post series (R00232, R00356) or a non-randomised bundle in which steroids are not the sole component (R01388). The directions are therefore not consistent across design tiers.

## 2. Zinc

- **Randomised evidence — 1 trial, with results:** R01955 (RCT; RoB 2 overall **High**). Zinc sulfate 220 mg (50 mg elemental zinc) twice daily vs no zinc, in 105 patients with olfactory dysfunction (49 vs 56). The printed between-group result is **composite**: median duration of recovery of gustatory and/or olfactory function was significantly shorter with zinc (p<0.001; no composite medians printed). Full-text check: the numerical durations 7 days (5–9) vs 18 days (14–22) are Table 5 **smell recovery only** — no taste-specific duration or recovery proportion is printed, so no taste-specific zinc effect estimate exists in this trial as published.
- **Observational / uncontrolled only:** R00323 — oral zinc (n=108, self-chosen) was associated with a **lower** chance of total (composite smell+taste) recovery, OR 0.24 (95% CI 0.1–0.5) (ROBINS-I **Serious**). R00232 combines zinc gluconate with prednisolone (uncontrolled; §1). R00782 — zinc ordered for adolescents with dysgeusia, number treated not printed, no taste-specific treatment outcome reported (ROBINS-I **Serious**). R02693 includes zinc 50 mg/day inside the TNT bundle (§7). R00334 lists zinc among survey treatments (§11).
- **Consistency of direction:** Inconsistent and non-comparable: a composite (not taste-specific) randomised signal in the direction of faster recovery (R01955, High RoB), an observational association in the opposite direction (R00323, Serious RoB), and uncontrolled uses with no taste-specific estimate (R00232, R00782). Adverse events for zinc are reported only within R00323 (oral zinc 13.9% of users reporting adverse events; see §10); R01955 and R00782 report no adverse-event data.

## 3. Photobiomodulation / low-level laser therapy

- **Randomised evidence — 1 trial, with results:** R00627 (RCT, 1:1, blinded; RoB 2 overall **Some concerns**). Single-session low-level diode laser to the tongue (settled dose: 940 nm, 1 W, 4 J/cm², 6 min total; the abstract conclusion refers to 810 nm — internal discrepancy as printed) vs placebo light, 18 vs 18, persistent taste loss ≥4 weeks. Complete taste restoration at 1 month: 17/18 (94.4%) laser (p<0.001 vs placebo); partial restoration was more frequent with placebo, 7/18 (38.9%) (p=0.041); 13/18 (72.2%) of the laser group had restored taste at 1 week vs 13/18 (72.2%) of the placebo group only at 4 weeks (p<0.001). Adverse events: **not reported**.
- **Uncontrolled pre-post only:** R01311 (PBMT 660 nm, 100 mW, 2 J/point, three session schedules; n=10; ROBINS-I **Serious**). Taste recovery "noted in all cases but with varying degrees of improvement"; per-protocol numbers and VAS values appear in a figure only; per-protocol group sizes are not printed (full-text checked). Adverse events: **not reported**.
- **Consistency of direction:** Both studies report improvement in the treated participants, but only R00627 has a comparator; R01311's pre-post change cannot be separated from natural history.

## 4. Stellate ganglion block (SGB)

- **No randomised or controlled evidence.** Three uncontrolled reports, all in persistent dysfunction:
  - R00625 (case series, n=195; ROBINS-I **Serious**): 74.4% (n=145) reported improvement in taste after right-sided SGB (first 24 hours; survey within 7 days); no complete-recovery proportion printed. Adverse events: not reported (miosis/ptosis documented as expected signs of successful blockade).
  - R00539 (case series, n=6; RoB **not assessable**, <10): 5/6 (83%) reported significant improvements in olfaction and gustation; per-case taste improvements of 80%, 90% then complete return, and cumulative 80% at 3 months are printed; transient side effects (ptosis, conjunctival erythema, dysphonia, dysphagia, hypopharyngeal anaesthesia) are reported.
  - R02954 (case report, n=1; RoB **not assessable**): altered taste resolved a few days after the second (left-sided) block. Adverse events: not reported.
- **Consistency of direction:** All three report improvement after the block, at very short follow-up (24 hours to ~3 months), with no comparator in any study — direction is uniform but entirely uncontrolled and largely self-reported.

## 5. Olfactory / gustatory training and related training protocols

- **No randomised evidence for training as the intervention.** (In C9634 and R03352, training was given to both arms as background, not tested.)
- **Uncontrolled pre-post:** C6826 (structured olfactory training, two modalities, final analysis n=25; ROBINS-I **Serious**): Taste Strips score 6.2 ± 4.1 at baseline to 7.2 ± 4.7 at 12 weeks, p=0.11 (Sniffin' Sticks subgroup p=0.34; patient-selected odorants p=0.19); no taste recovery proportion printed. Adverse events: not reported.
- **Non-randomised controlled (bundle):** R01388 (§1) — training is part of the bundle that showed 88.6% vs 63.9% recovery to normal taste; the training component cannot be isolated.
- **Uncontrolled multi-component:** R02693 (TNT, n=5; §7) and R00537 (camera-based visual feedback training, n=15 completers; ROBINS-I **Serious**): subjective loss-of-taste rating 3.20 (SD 1.97) pre vs 1.00 (SD 1.07) post (p<0.001); 11/13 participants with taste impairment showed reduced severity; objective ODOFIN taste test 3.12 (0.99) vs 3.87 (0.35), p=0.08 (n=8 paired). Adverse events: not reported.
- **Observational only:** R00574 (self-practised odor sniffing in 42 patients "provided no beneficial effect", no rates), R01391 (olfactory training recorded separately; recovery stated to be unrelated to treatment or training), R00334 (smell training in the flagged survey; §11).
- **Consistency of direction:** Mixed — a non-significant objective taste-score change in the one prospective pre-post cohort (C6826), positive subjective changes in uncontrolled R00537/R02693, and null statements in the two large observational datasets (R00574, R01391).

## 6. Cerebrolysin

- **Randomised design, no results:** R03352 (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06208540; registry record, randomised, parallel, open-label, phase 4; enrolment 160 actual; RoB **not assessable** — no results posted, hasResults: false). Cerebrolysin 5 ml IM once daily, 5×/week, 20 treatments per cycle, repeated ≥6 to ≤24 weeks, plus olfactory/gustatory training in both arms vs training only. **There is no effect estimate and no adverse-event data for cerebrolysin in the settled set.**

## 7. Multi-component / combination protocols (not classifiable to a single agent)

- R00356 — corticosteroid-based combination protocol (§1); uncontrolled, n=87, taste VAS 3.2 → 7.1 at 12 months.
- R02693 — Training 'N' Treatment (TNT), retrospective case series n=5 (RoB **not assessable**, <10): training plus zinc, vitamins A/D3, alpha-lipoic acid, B-complex, saline irrigation, fluticasone, nerve-stimulation exercises, with a phase-2 escalation (triamcinolone paste, sodium citrate spray, theophylline spray, prednisone). Complete recovery of taste (and smell) in 5/5 (100%), all by day 37; taste score 10% at baseline to 58% at ~10 days (p=0.0002). Adverse events: "No adverse events were reported during treatment with the TNT protocol" (as printed). Acute-phase initiation (within 48 hours of symptom onset, as printed) means spontaneous recovery is a competing explanation the design cannot exclude.
- R01388 — training + medical bundle (§1/§5), the only one of the three with a comparator group.

## 8. Observational treatment-exposure studies (treatments not assigned by the study)

- R00323 (cross-sectional, n=714; ROBINS-I **Serious**): self-chosen acute-phase drugs vs no treatment (437 vs 277). Total (composite smell+taste) recovery was **lower** in treated than untreated participants: 122/437 (27.9%) vs 127/277 (45.8%). No medication had a clear positive effect; oral zinc OR 0.24 (95% CI 0.1–0.5) for total recovery; nitazoxanide OR 3.30 (95% CI 1.35–8.20), not significant after Bonferroni correction. This is a controlled comparison in form, but exposure was self-chosen and confounding by indication applies (RoB rationale, table).
- R01391 (prospective cohort, n=1,031; ROBINS-I **Serious**): routine-care categories — intranasal steroids 312, systemic steroids 62, omega-3 104, others 305, combinations 41, no treatment 207. No per-treatment recovery proportions are printed; the paper's conclusion (full-text checked) is that most recovery occurred in the first two weeks and was unrelated to demographics, treatment, or olfactory training.
- R00574 (cross-sectional, n=185; JBI cross-sectional, overall **Moderate**): 146 (78.92%) had received nasal irrigations, local/systemic steroids, or vitamins/supplements (including zinc, omega-3, L-carnitine and others); 39 (21.08%) had no consultation or treatment. The paper states no modality was effective and self-practised odor sniffing gave no benefit; no per-treatment rates are printed, so no treatment-effect comparison can be extracted.
- R02506 (cross-sectional, n=417; JBI cross-sectional, overall **Low** for the survey's own questions): the entire gustatory-treatment content is one sentence (full-text checked) — 4 patients (1.4%) received L-carnitine or trace elements and vitamins for gustatory dysfunction. No dose, course, comparator, outcome, or adverse-event data are printed. This row is an evidence-map entry only; it contributes no effect estimate.

## 9. Single-case and very small reports (no synthesis weight)

- R01519 (n=1, age 87; RoB **not assessable**): pregabalin 25 mg every 12 hours for persistent metallic taste — improvement at 2 months without the metallic perception disappearing completely. The only adverse-event content in this row concerns the previously tried clonazepam (somnolence, poor tolerance in an older adult).
- R01378 (n=2; RoB **not assessable**): single-dose turmeric supplement (1000 mg turmeric extract with black pepper extract; subject 2 also Boswellia) — subject 1 self-rated smell/taste 1/10 → 10/10 ten minutes after the dose on day 46; subject 2 1/10 → 6/10 at 12 hours and 10/10 at 3 days after a day-4 dose. Adverse events: not reported; the paper notes curcumin inhibition of cytochrome P450 enzymes as a caution (special-populations column).
- R02883 (coffee/caffeine; ROBINS-I **Serious**): non-randomised coffee vs no-coffee comparison reporting combined smell+taste improvement of 98.3% vs 1.3% (diabetes+hypertension), 96.6% vs 1.4% (heart disease+diabetes+hypertension), 98.2% vs 1.1% (heart disease+diabetes), 98.4% vs 1.2% (heart disease+hypertension) and 98.5% vs 1.3% (no underlying disease). Per-arm numbers are not reported (118,341 "valid cases" in Table 3, as printed), the coffee dose is internally inconsistent in the paper (mg vs grams, as settled), and adverse events are not reported. The figures are reproduced exactly as printed and carry Serious risk of bias on every ROBINS-I domain in the adjudicated assessment.
- R02954 (n=1 SGB) and R00539 (n=6 SGB) are covered in §4; R02693 (n=5 TNT) in §7.

## 10. Adverse-event reporting completeness

Of the **23 rows, adverse-event content is present in 6 and "not reported" in 17**:

- **Present (6):** C9634 (full comparative reporting: 3 adverse events in the prednisolone group — sleeplessness with psychological disorders requiring deblinding, restlessness with treatment stopped, stomach irritation requiring omeprazole; mild side-effects 9/58 (15.5%) prednisolone vs 5/57 (8.8%) placebo; no serious adverse events; 3 stopped treatment because of side-effects); R00323 (55/483 (11.4%) of medication users reported adverse events; highest with chloroquine/hydroxychloroquine 22.4%, azithromycin 14.3%, oral zinc 13.9%; most common upper abdominal pain and nausea; 4 subjects (0.8%) cardiac arrhythmia; no other severe adverse events reported); R00539 (transient SGB effects listed in §4); R00356 ("No safety problems were identified and the treatment was always well-tolerated", as printed — a statement, with no event counts); R02693 ("No adverse events were reported during treatment", as printed — statement only); R01519 (somnolence on clonazepam, the prior drug, in an 87-year-old; none reported for pregabalin).
- **Not reported (17):** R00232, R00334, R00537, R00574, R00625, R00627, R00782, R01311, R01378, R01388, R01391, R01955, R02506, R02883, R02954, R03352, C6826. This includes two of the four randomised studies (R00627, R03352) and the zinc RCT (R01955). Two rows carry adjacent non-AE notes that are not adverse-event reporting: R00537 (a participant's body-image concern, not labelled an adverse event by the paper) and R00625 (miosis/ptosis documented as expected signs of a successful block).

## 11. Flagged row — treatment-use survey, no effect estimate (R00334)

R00334 (Murphy 2024; JBI analytical cross-sectional, overall **Moderate**) is a patient survey (USA Smell and Taste Patient Survey; 6,100 completed, 4,877 analysed; mixed aetiology — COVID and other causes) in which **1,890 respondents reported past use of treatments they or their prescribers had chosen**: nasal steroids, oral steroids, zinc, nasal rinse, smell training, theophylline, platelet-rich plasma, omega-3 (full-text checked; nasal steroids 43% and smell training 31% of treatment users; smell training users n=790). Dose/course: not reported (the paper states complete information on duration of steroid use or olfactory training was not available). **There is no untreated comparator, no assigned intervention, no baseline/follow-up outcome measurement, and no effect estimate.** The only outcome is retrospective self-rated efficacy: nasal steroids were rated slightly effective or not effective at all by 91% of users aged 40–60 and 93% of those 60+; smell training by 91% (40–60) and 89% (60+); only respondents under 40 reported more than slight efficacy of steroids or smell training; age was the most consistent predictor of rated efficacy. Adverse events: not reported. This row is evidence about treatment *use and perceived efficacy only* and is excluded from every comparative statement above except where explicitly cited as survey data.

## 12. Summary counts (all traceable to the table)

- **Randomised evidence:** 4 studies are randomised in design — R00627 (laser), R01955 (zinc), C9634 (prednisolone), R03352 (cerebrolysin) — covering **4 intervention groups**. One of the four (R03352) has **no results posted**, so **3 intervention groups have any randomised effect estimate**: photobiomodulation/low-level laser, zinc (composite smell/taste outcome only, High RoB), and oral prednisolone (no gustatory effect, Low RoB).
- **Any controlled comparison at all:** **8 of the 23 studies** have a comparator group of any kind — R00323 (observational, vs no treatment), R00627 (placebo), R01388 (non-randomised, vs conventional management), R01391 (routine-care categories incl. a no-treatment group), R01955 (vs no zinc), R02883 (non-randomised, vs no coffee), R03352 (registry design only, no results), C9634 (placebo). The remaining **15 studies are uncontrolled pre-post, single-arm, single-case, or exposure-descriptive only** (including R00574, where an untreated subgroup is described but no treatment comparison is printed, and R00334, which has no comparator).
- **Design/RoB profile of the whole set:** adjudicated overall RoB is Low for 2 studies (C9634, RoB 2; and R02506, Low on the JBI cross-sectional tool for its survey questions but contributing no treatment estimate), Some concerns for 1 (R00627), High for 1 (R01955), Moderate for 2 (R00334, R00574 — both JBI cross-sectional), Serious for 11 (all ROBINS-I), and not assessable for 6 (R00539, R01378, R01519, R02693, R02954, R03352 — case reports/series <10 or a results-free registry record). (Count check, code-verified: 2+1+1+2+11+6 = 23.)
- **Special populations:** treatment data in children/adolescents exist only in R00782 (adolescents 12–17, no taste-specific outcome) and as eligibility in R03352 (ages 10–70, no results); pregnancy was an exclusion in C9634 and R00627; diabetes treated with drugs was an exclusion in C9634, while R02883 dosed by underlying-disease groups including diabetes; the only older-adult treatment report is the n=1 pregabalin case (R01519, age 87). No study in the set reports a dose adjustment for any special population.
