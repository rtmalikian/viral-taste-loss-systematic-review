## Footnotes (downgrades applied)

- ᵃ **Risk of bias (−1):** majority of studies Moderate or worse on adjudicated JBI/RoB assessment; taste and recovery predominantly self-reported without validated instruments; for time-to-recovery, durations often retrospective recall and conditioned on having recovered.
- ᵇ **Inconsistency (−2 for pooled proportions; −1 for time-to-recovery narrative):** I² 88.1–99.6% across the proportion pools; 95% prediction intervals span 17.3–99.6% (recovery) and 5.3–86.7% (frequency) — a new comparable study could find almost any value. Subgroups (ascertainment, setting, low-RoB sensitivity) do not resolve it. Any-recovery >12 weeks is the exception: I² 44.1%, PI 78.6–95.9%, no inconsistency downgrade.
- ᶜ **Indirectness (−1; −2 for time-to-recovery):** all evidence is SARS-CoV-2 while the review question covers viral infections generally — no data for influenza or any other virus in any row. Frequency additionally: self-reported "taste" liable to olfactory misattribution (only 15/137 psychophysical). Recovery windows pool non-uniform assessment timepoints. Time-to-recovery: estimand shifts across studies and much duration data is composite smell/taste (−2).
- ᵈ **Imprecision (−1):** recovery pools are small (k = 5–11; N = 325–2,085) with pooled CIs ≈15–26 percentage points wide; time-to-recovery has no pooled estimate or CI.
- ᵉ **Indirectness (−1) — prednisolone:** cohort sampled on persistent *olfactory* disorders; gustatory function a secondary outcome; no recovery proportion reported (rating covers score outcomes only); prednisolone tested as add-on to olfactory training in both arms.
- ᶠ **Imprecision (−1):** single trial; optimal information size for the review's critical (recovery) outcomes not met; no replication.
- ᵍ **Risk of bias (−1) — photobiomodulation:** RoB 2 Some concerns (randomisation process); internal wavelength discrepancy (940 nm methods vs 810 nm abstract conclusion).
- ʰ **Imprecision (−2) — photobiomodulation:** single trial, n = 36 (18/arm); headline proportion rests on 17 events in one arm; comparator complete-restoration count not printed, so no comparative CI can be constructed.
- ⁱ **Risk of bias (−2) — zinc:** RoB 2 overall High; measurement of outcome High (unblinded physician/self-report, no validated gustatory test, open, no placebo, no ITT); randomisation and selective-reporting domains Some concerns; no registration/protocol.
- ʲ **Indirectness (−1) — zinc composite row:** outcome is a smell-and/or-taste composite in a cohort selected for olfactory dysfunction; cannot isolate a taste effect.
- ᵏ **Risk of bias / reporting bias (−1, floor):** AE content absent in 17/23 studies (74%), including 3 of 4 randomised studies' designs with no results or no AE reporting (R00627, R01955, R03352); ascertainment unsystematic where present; two of six "present" sources are unquantified statements.
- ˡ **Indirectness (−1, recorded):** the only multi-user AE rates concern self-chosen acute-COVID drug exposures, not assigned taste treatments; the only comparative source covers one 10-day prednisolone course.
- ᵐ **Imprecision (−1, recorded):** total comparative safety denominator 115 randomised participants (one trial); serious events are rare-event outcomes; a zero count at this size cannot exclude important harm; 22/23 studies provide no usable safety denominator for discontinuation.
- Publication bias: not formally assessable for any row (no funnel/small-study analysis in the analysis set; uninterpretable at I² ≈ 99% for proportion pools; single trials for treatments) — not downgraded except as documented non-reporting in ᵏ.
- Upgrading: none applied to any row. The large photobiomodulation effect was not upgraded (single small RCT with RoB concerns; large-effect upgrading applies to observational bodies).
